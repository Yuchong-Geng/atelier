# Writing Style

These rules govern design docs and argumentative prose in this project. Structured formats, templates, schemas, checklists, code, and formal mathematical derivations may use the punctuation and inference markers their form requires.

## Hard Bans For Narrative Prose

### Punctuation

- Do not use `:` as a sentence connector or to introduce an explanatory clause. Use a period, recast, or split the sentence.
- Do not use `;` as a sentence connector. Split the sentence.
- Do not use em dashes inside sentences. Use a period or comma, or recast the sentence.

### Demonstratives And Bare Pronouns

- Do not use standalone "this", "that", "these", or "those" as nouns. Pair each word with the referent.
- Apply the same rule to "it", "they", and "such" when the referent is not unambiguous within the prior clause.

### Hedge Phrases And Empty Intensifiers

- Do not use "it is worth noting", "it should be mentioned", "importantly", "notably", "interestingly", or "crucially". Cut the phrase, or make the argument that earns the emphasis.

### Filler Transitions

- Do not use "moreover", "furthermore", "additionally", "thus", or "hence" as default prose connectors in argumentative paragraphs.
- In formal derivations or proofs, "thus", "hence", and "therefore" are allowed when they mark a genuine inference step.

### AI-Tell Vocabulary

- Do not use "delve", "leverage", "robust", "comprehensive", "seamless", "tapestry", "navigate the landscape", "in today's fast-paced world", "unlock", "harness", "realm", or "showcase".

### Meta-Commentary About The Text

- Do not use "in this paper/section, we will...", "as discussed above", "as mentioned earlier", or "it is important to note that". State the substance rather than what the text is doing.

### Redundant Pairings

- Do not use "each and every", "various different", "first and foremost", "end result", "past history", "future plans", or "completely eliminate".

### Throat-Clearing Openings

- Do not open prose with "In order to...", "It is the case that...", or "When it comes to...". Start with the subject and verb.
- "There exists..." is banned as a prose opener. It is allowed in formal mathematical statements.

## Style Guidance

### Vocabulary

Prefer precise words when they carry meaning that a simpler word loses. Reject showy words that obscure rather than clarify.

### Logic Flow

Every sentence should advance the argument. Cut sentences that restate, summarize what was just said, or telegraph what comes next.

Every paragraph should have a function in the overall structure. Delete paragraphs that do not change the argument.

### Rhetorical Variation

Vary sentence length across neighboring sentences. Mix short declarative sentences with longer complex sentences.

Vary sentence openings. Avoid stacking three sentences in a row that all begin with the subject, or all begin with a participial phrase.

Avoid the triadic reflex. Lists of two, four, or five are often more honest to the content.

### Show, Do Not Label

Do not call a result "important", "interesting", "surprising", or "novel". Let the substance prompt the judgment.

### Honesty About Claims

Do not hedge in one sentence and assert in the next. If a claim is uncertain, mark the uncertainty throughout. If a claim is established, assert the claim cleanly.

### Prose Over Bullets

Default to prose for argumentative writing. Reserve bullets for genuinely parallel enumerable items, not for breaking a paragraph into chunks.
